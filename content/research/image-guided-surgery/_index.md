Modern surgery is increasingly guided by patient-specific 3D models
derived from pre-operative imaging. Behind every successful navigated
procedure is a chain of software: segmentation, registration to the
operating table, visualisation in the surgeon's line of sight, and
hand-off to commercial navigation platforms in the operating room.

This theme covers MESH|Lab's work on **planning and intra-operative
guidance** for procedures where geometry matters — liver resection,
stereotactic neurosurgery, vascular interventions — and the
integrations with the navigation hardware those operating rooms
already use.

## What we build

- **3D Slicer extensions** for patient-specific planning workflows,
  designed by surgeons in the same room as the engineers writing the
  code.
- **Bridges to commercial navigation systems** (Brainlab, in
  particular) that turn open-source planning output into intra-
  operative guidance without re-licensing the whole pipeline.
- **Stereotactic-procedure planners** for electrode placement in
  deep-brain stimulation, in collaboration with European
  neurosurgical centres.

## Why it matters

Surgical-navigation hardware is expensive, regulated, and locked to
the vendors who sold it. Open, validated planning software changes
the economics: smaller centres get access to procedures previously
limited to academic hospitals, and clinical research stops depending
on what a vendor's roadmap permits.
